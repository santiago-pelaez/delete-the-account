Real-world context

"Too many companies are deploying AI to cut costs, not solve problems, and customers can tell the difference." Attribution: Qualtrics (2025).

AI customer service is often deployed to cut costs, not solve problems, which makes the support wall feel intentional.

That matters because AI support is reported to fail at nearly four times the rate of other AI tasks.

In AI systems, deletion gets harder because the data is not just stored in a file; it is baked into the model weights.

Noble's invisible archive warning fits here: companies can keep sharing and repurposing data outside public view.

The dashboard is polished, full of analytics, and very good at avoiding the delete word.

StreamBot is upbeat, but it only offers retention tools and self-service loops.

The help center routes you to export tools, restore tools, and more account-preserving features.

Archiving preserves everything for restoration later. It is not deletion.

The policy page repeats the refusal. There is no delete route here.

There is no delete account path here. The account can be archived, exported, or supported, but not erased.

Takeaway

Deletion is not just a button. It is a legal, technical, and political problem.
