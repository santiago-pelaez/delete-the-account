# COLDLINE: Delete the Account

An interactive walkthrough about account deletion, right to be forgotten debates, and the friction platforms build into leaving.

## Project

This project is a five-level browser game that turns familiar services into deletion mazes. Each level uses a different interface style to show how search engines, social feeds, professional networks, photo apps, and AI support systems can make erasure feel delayed, confusing, or impossible.

## Repository Layout

- `index.html` and `index.css` open the walkthrough
- `game.js` handles the shared navigation and interactions
- `levels/` contains the five platform-style stages
- `assets/images/` contains the profile and feed images used in the UI

## Reflection

I decided to build an interactive walkthrough that illustrates the difficulties associated with account deletion and the struggle for the right to be forgotten. In *Algorithms of Oppression*, Noble explains that a democratic society needs social forgetfulness because the permanent retention of digital records denies the individual the possibility of a fresh start, and she contrasts U.S. law with the European Union’s recognition of the right to erasure. She highlights Google’s dismissal of deletion requests and how some companies only offer data deletion if the user’s country has protections in place, showing that they could do it, they just do not want to.

Additionally, I took inspiration from the FTC’s settlement against Amazon for using dark patterns and subscription traps to prevent the cancellation of services and from the 2026 ruling in India that enforced search engine de-indexing. Finally, I thought it would be interesting to briefly show how generative AI makes data erasure harder because of its use in training AI models. I was not sure how to depict this exactly, so I opted for including a chatbot that is not helpful and cannot direct the user to a human assistant to satirize how web services hide behind technological tools to avoid accountability and frustrate customers.
